# Car Purchase Comparison Notes

Notes comparing options before deciding whether to buy a newer car or keep the current one running.

## Option 1: Buy a Used Sedan

Buying the used sedan outright would cost $18,500, while financing it over 48 months adds roughly $1,900 in interest.
The sedan has 32,000 miles and a remaining two years on the manufacturer warranty.
A pre-purchase inspection at an independent mechanic is budgeted at $120 before making an offer.

## Option 2: Trade In the Current Automobile

The dealership's trade-in offer for the old automobile was $4,200, about $800 below the online private-sale estimate.
Selling privately would take more time and effort but could net closer to $5,000 after minor repairs.
The current automobile has 118,000 miles and needs new brake pads soon regardless of whether it is sold or kept.

## Option 3: Keep the Current Car

Keeping the current car avoids any new purchase but means budgeting for a $600 brake job and likely other repairs within the year.
The car has no remaining warranty coverage, so any future repair is a full out-of-pocket cost.

## Insurance Impact

Insurance quotes for the newer car came in at $1,380 per year, compared to $980 for keeping the current vehicle.
The $400 difference in annual insurance cost should be added to the total cost of ownership when comparing the two options.

## Decision Factors

Monthly cash flow favors keeping the current car, but the higher repair risk makes the total cost less predictable over the next two years.
The plan is to get one more mechanic estimate before deciding, since the brake job quote came from only one shop so far.
