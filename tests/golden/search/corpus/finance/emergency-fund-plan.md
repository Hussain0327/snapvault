# Emergency Fund Plan

A written plan for building an emergency fund, started after a stretch of living paycheck to paycheck last year.

## Target

The goal is to build an emergency fund covering six months of essential expenses, roughly $16,800 in total.
Essential expenses were calculated as rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, and minimum debt payments, excluding discretionary spending.
The six-month target was chosen over a more common three-month target because income from freelance work is unpredictable.

## Funding

Automatic transfers of $300 per month move from checking into a separate high-yield savings account.
Any freelance income above $500 in a given month also gets split, with half going directly into the emergency fund.
Tax refunds and other one-time windfalls are earmarked entirely for the fund until the target is reached.

## Timeline

At the current pace, the fund will reach its full target in approximately 14 months from the plan's start date.
The plan started with $4,200 already saved, so the automatic transfers only need to cover the remaining $12,600.
A mid-point check-in is scheduled for month seven to confirm the pace is still on track.

## Rules for Use

The fund is reserved strictly for job loss, medical emergencies, or urgent home and car repairs, not planned purchases.
Any withdrawal must be repaid through increased contributions within three months, doubling the normal $300 monthly transfer if needed.
A withdrawal log will track the date, amount, and reason for every time the fund is used.

## Account Choice

The fund lives in a high-yield savings account currently paying 4.3 percent annual interest, kept separate from everyday checking to reduce the temptation to spend it.
