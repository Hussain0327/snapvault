# Lawn Sprinkler Winterization Notes

Steps for shutting down the irrigation system before the first freeze each year.

## Timing

Winterizing the sprinkler system involves blowing out each of the six zones with compressed air at 50 PSI.
The system should be winterized before the first hard freeze, typically around November 1st in this area.
Watching the extended forecast in late October helps decide the exact weekend to schedule the work.

## Procedure

The main water supply to the irrigation system is shut off first, at the valve near the meter.
An air compressor is connected to the system's blowout port, and each zone is run individually until only air comes out of the sprinkler heads.
Running compressed air through too many zones at once can damage the valves, so zones are always done one at a time.

## Equipment

A rented air compressor rated for at least 80 CFM works better than the smaller home compressor tried in the first year.
Safety glasses are worn during the process, since sprinkler heads can spit out debris along with the water and air.

## Risks of Skipping

Skipping winterization risks cracked pipes, which cost $380 to repair last year after a freeze in zone three.
Water left in the pipes expands as it freezes, and the resulting cracks often are not discovered until the system is turned back on in spring.
The backflow preventer above ground is especially vulnerable and is insulated separately with a foam cover each winter.

## Spring Startup

In spring, the system is turned back on gradually, one zone at a time, to check for leaks before running everything at full pressure.
Any sprinkler heads that were damaged over winter are typically found and replaced during this spring startup check.
