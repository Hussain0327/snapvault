# Water Heater Annual Inspection Record

Notes from the yearly checkup on the water heater in the second property, kept separate from the main house's maintenance log.

## The Visit

The annual inspection of the 50-gallon water heater on February 8th found no leaks and normal sediment buildup.
The technician spent about 45 minutes checking the pressure relief valve, the thermostat, and the venting.
This unit is now 4 years old and shows no signs of the corrosion issues that led to the other property's replacement.

## Findings

The technician flushed the tank and recommended replacing the anode rod within the next two years.
Water pressure at the tank measured 62 PSI, within the normal range and unchanged from last year's reading.
The pressure relief valve was tested and released cleanly, confirming it is not stuck or corroded.

## Maintenance Plan

This inspection, part of a $95 annual maintenance plan, is scheduled every February to catch problems early.
The plan also covers a discount on any parts replacement, including the anode rod recommended this year.
Signing up for the plan two years ago has kept this unit in noticeably better condition than the one that failed at the other property.

## Cost Comparison

At $95 per year, five years of inspections cost less than the $1,450 spent replacing the other property's failed unit.
The anode rod replacement, if done next year, is estimated at an additional $85 including labor.

## Next Steps

Schedule the anode rod replacement for next February's visit rather than waiting the full two years the technician suggested.
Keep this inspection on the same February schedule each year to track sediment and pressure trends over time.
