# Water Heater Replacement Record

Notes from replacing the failing water heater in the basement utility closet.

## The Failure

The 40-gallon water heater was replaced on May 14th after the original unit started leaking from the bottom seam.
The leak was first noticed as a small puddle on May 10th, which grew enough by May 12th to justify calling a plumber immediately.
The original unit was 11 years old, close to the end of its typical service life.

## The Replacement

The new unit is a Rheem model XG40T06EC36U0, installed by a licensed technician for a total of $1,450.
Installation took about four hours, including draining and removing the old tank and connecting the new gas line and vent.
The technician also replaced the shutoff valve, which had corroded and was difficult to close fully.

## Warranty

The replacement includes a 6-year warranty on the tank and a 1-year warranty on labor from the installer.
The warranty card and receipt are stored in the home maintenance binder along with the model and serial number.
Registering the warranty online within 90 days is required to keep the full 6-year tank coverage active.

## Settings

The thermostat on the new unit is set to 120 degrees Fahrenheit, matching the previous unit's setting for safety and efficiency.
A drip pan with a drain line was added underneath the new tank in case of a future leak.

## Maintenance Going Forward

Draining a few gallons from the tank every six months is recommended to reduce sediment buildup and extend the unit's life.
The anode rod should be checked around year three and likely replaced by year five, per the installer's advice.
