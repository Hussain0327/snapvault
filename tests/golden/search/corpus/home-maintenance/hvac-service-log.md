# HVAC Service Log

A record of furnace and air conditioner maintenance for the house, kept to track warranty coverage and recurring issues.

## Filter Changes

The furnace filter, a 16x25x1 pleated filter, was replaced on April 2nd after three months of use.
A second filter change is logged for July 5th, and the next one is due around October.
Switching to a higher-MERV filter last year seems to have reduced dust buildup on the vents noticeably.

## Spring Service

The technician found the outdoor condenser unit's refrigerant level 1.5 pounds low and topped it off during the June visit.
The evaporator coil was cleaned during the same visit, which had visible dust buildup from the previous owner's neglect.
No leaks were detected in the refrigerant lines, so the low level was attributed to a slow, unmeasured loss over several years.

## Fall Service

The fall inspection checked the heat exchanger for cracks and tested the ignition system, both of which passed without issue.
Carbon monoxide levels near the furnace were tested and came back within the normal safe range.

## Costs

Annual HVAC service costs $185 for the spring inspection and $165 for the fall inspection under the current contract.
The refrigerant top-off in June was an extra $95 beyond the standard inspection fee, since it required additional labor.
The service contract renews each January and locks in the current pricing for another twelve months.

## Notes for Next Year

Ask the technician about upgrading the thermostat to a smart model during the next spring visit, since the current one is over eight years old.
Keep an eye on the refrigerant level at the next visit in case the low reading in June indicates a slow leak developing.
