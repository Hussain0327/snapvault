# Car Rental Notes for Driving in Italy

Notes gathered before renting an automobile for the first time in Europe.

## Booking

The rental automobile, a compact Fiat 500, costs 38 euros per day with unlimited mileage included.
The booking was made through a comparison site rather than directly with the rental counter, which saved about 15 percent.
A credit card hold of 300 euros is placed at pickup as a damage deposit and released within two weeks after return.

## Requirements

International driving permits are required in Italy in addition to a valid US driver's license.
The permit must be obtained before departure; it cannot be issued once already in the country.
Drivers must be at least 21 years old, and drivers under 25 pay a young driver surcharge of 12 euros per day.

## Insurance

Basic collision coverage is included in the rental price, but it carries a 1,200 euro deductible if the automobile is damaged.
Purchasing the supplemental insurance through the rental company adds 18 euros per day and reduces the deductible to zero.
Some US credit cards include rental car coverage automatically, which is worth checking before paying for extra insurance.

## Driving Notes

Many Italian city centers restrict non-resident vehicles in zones marked ZTL, and fines arrive by mail months later.
Fuel is sold by the liter, and diesel and gasoline pumps look different enough that it is worth double-checking before fueling.
Automatic transmission cars cost more to rent than manual ones, so it is worth booking well in advance if an automatic is required.

## Return

Return the car with a full tank; the rental company charges 2.50 euros per liter for any shortfall.
Photograph the exterior of the car at pickup and drop-off to document its condition in case of a dispute.
The return counter closes at 9:00 PM, so a late flight arrival may mean an after-hours drop box instead.
