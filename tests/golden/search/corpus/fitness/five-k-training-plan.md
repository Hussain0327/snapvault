# 5K Training Plan

This 8-week plan takes a runner from a comfortable 3-mile jog to racing a 5K at a steady pace.
It assumes three runs a week plus one optional cross-training day.

## Weeks 1-3

Run three times a week: two easy 25-minute runs and one slightly longer 35-minute run on the weekend.
Keep your pace slow enough that you could hold a conversation the whole time.

## Weeks 4-6

Introduce one interval session per week: six repeats of 400 meters at a faster pace than race pace, with a 90-second jog between each repeat.
The weekend long run grows to 45 minutes by the end of week 6.

## Weeks 7-8

Reduce total volume by about 20 percent to taper before race day, but keep one short interval session to stay sharp.
Race week itself should include only two short easy runs before the 5K on Saturday.

## Target pace

Most runners finishing this plan complete a 5K in 28 to 32 minutes, depending on their starting fitness.
Track your pace with a phone app or a basic GPS watch; a stopwatch alone makes the interval sessions hard to pace correctly.

## Shoes and gear

Replace running shoes every 500 to 800 kilometers, since worn midsoles are a common cause of shin splints in new runners.
A doctor visit is worth scheduling if any joint pain lasts more than three days without improving.

## Nutrition

Eat a small carbohydrate snack, such as a banana, about an hour before any run longer than 30 minutes.
Hydrate with roughly 500 milliliters of water in the two hours before a long run or race.
