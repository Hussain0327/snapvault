# 10K Training Plan

This 12-week plan is for a runner who has already completed a 5K and wants to move up to racing a 10K.
It uses four runs a week instead of three.

## Weeks 1-4

Build a base with four runs: three easy runs of 30 to 40 minutes and one weekend long run starting at 50 minutes.
Add five minutes to the long run every other week.

## Weeks 5-8

Add a tempo run midweek: 20 minutes at a pace about 30 seconds per kilometer slower than 10K race pace.
The weekend long run reaches 80 minutes by the end of week 8, which is the longest single session in the plan.

## Weeks 9-11

Introduce hill repeats once a week: eight repeats of a 200-meter incline, jogging back down for recovery between each one.
Keep the long run steady at 80 minutes rather than extending it further this late in the plan.

## Week 12

Taper hard: cut total weekly volume by roughly a third and skip hill repeats entirely during race week.
The 10K itself falls on the Sunday of week 12.

## Target pace

Runners who complete this plan typically finish a 10K in 58 to 65 minutes.
That is noticeably slower per kilometer than 5K pace, which is expected over double the distance.

## Injury notes

Shin and knee soreness that appears after the tempo runs in weeks 5-8 is common and usually resolves with two easy days; soreness lasting beyond a week should prompt a visit to a physician rather than pushing through it.

## Gear

A GPS watch is more useful here than for a 5K, since holding tempo pace by feel alone over 20 minutes is difficult for most runners below advanced level.
