# Sourdough Starter Care Notes

Kept on the kitchen counter since we adopted this culture from a neighbor two summers ago.
The starter is a simple mix of flour and water left to ferment with wild yeast and bacteria from the air.

## Feeding Schedule

Feed the starter with 50 grams of flour and 50 grams of water every 24 hours at room temperature.
Use a blend of whole wheat and unbleached bread flour for the best rise.
Discard about half the starter before each feeding so the jar does not overflow onto the counter.

## Signs of Health

A mature starter doubles in size within 4 to 6 hours after a feeding.
It should smell pleasantly sour, like plain yogurt, not sharp like nail polish remover.
Bubbles across the surface and a domed top both indicate active fermentation.

## Storage

Store the starter in the refrigerator and feed it once every 7 days if you bake less than weekly.
Bring it back to room temperature and feed it twice, 8 hours apart, before using it in a loaf.
A layer of dark liquid on top, called hooch, means the starter is hungry and needs feeding soon.

## Troubleshooting

If the starter stops rising after several days, discard most of it and switch to fresh flour.
Chlorinated tap water can slow fermentation, so filtered or bottled water works better for feedings.
Warmer kitchens speed things up; in midsummer, feedings every 12 hours may be needed instead of every 24.

## Using It in Bread

This starter powers our regular sourdough loaf, which bakes at 220 degrees Celsius for 40 minutes with the lid on.
Always test readiness with the float test: a spoonful dropped in a glass of water should float within a minute.
Skip a feeding right before a big bake and the loaf will taste noticeably more sour and dense.
