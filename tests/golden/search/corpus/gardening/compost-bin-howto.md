# How to Build and Maintain a Compost Bin

This guide covers building a simple three-bin compost system and keeping it productive through the year.

## Building the bins

Build three adjoining bins from pressure-treated lumber, each about 1 meter wide, 1 meter deep, and 1 meter tall.
Leave gaps of about 2 centimeters between the front boards for airflow, and make the front boards removable so finished compost is easy to shovel out.

## Starting a pile

Layer brown material, such as dry leaves or shredded cardboard, with green material, such as vegetable scraps or grass clippings, at roughly a 3-to-1 ratio by volume.
A pile that smells sour or ammonia-like usually has too much green material and needs more browns mixed in.

## Turning

Turn the pile every 1 to 2 weeks with a garden fork, moving material from the first bin into the second to introduce oxygen and speed decomposition.
A well-turned pile can reach an internal temperature of 60 degrees Celsius at its core within the first week, hot enough to kill most weed seeds.

## What to add

Add kitchen scraps, coffee grounds, eggshells, and yard trimmings.
Avoid meat, dairy, and oily foods, which attract pests and slow decomposition compared to plant-based scraps.

## What to avoid

Diseased plant material and weeds that have already gone to seed should be kept out, since the pile in this system does not reliably reach the 70 degrees Celsius needed to kill every weed seed and pathogen.

## Moisture

Keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out sponge; add water during dry weeks and cover with a tarp during heavy rain to avoid waterlogging.
A pile that is too wet becomes compacted and smells of ammonia rather than breaking down evenly.

## Finished compost

Compost is ready to use after 3 to 4 months in this three-bin system, noticeably faster than a single unturned pile, which can take a full year.
Finished compost should look dark and crumbly with no recognizable scraps remaining.
