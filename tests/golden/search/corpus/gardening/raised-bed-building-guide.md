# Raised Bed Building Guide

Instructions for building a single 1.2 meter by 2.4 meter raised garden bed from cedar boards, filled and ready to plant in a weekend.

## Materials

Four cedar boards, 5 centimeters by 30 centimeters by 2.4 meters, form the long sides, cut to length for the short sides from a fifth board.
Cedar resists rot better than pine and does not need chemical treatment, which matters for a bed growing food.

## Assembly

Screw the boards together at each corner using 10-centimeter deck screws, three per corner joint, and add a corner post cut from a 5 by 5 centimeter stake for extra rigidity.
The finished frame stands about 30 centimeters tall, enough depth for most root vegetables.

## Siting

Choose a spot that gets at least 6 hours of direct sun daily; less than that noticeably reduced tomato and pepper yields in a bed placed too close to the fence last year.
Level the ground before placing the frame, since a sloped bed drains unevenly and pools water on the low end.

## Lining

Line the bottom with cardboard to suppress grass before filling, which breaks down within the first season and does not need to be removed later.
Skip landscape fabric on the bottom, since it can interfere with drainage into the native soil below.

## Filling

Fill with a mix of two-thirds high-quality topsoil and one-third compost by volume; this bed took about 550 liters of mix to fill completely.
Top off with 5 centimeters of mulch to retain moisture and reduce weed growth.

## Cost

Materials for this bed cost about 95 dollars: 55 dollars for the cedar boards, 15 dollars for screws and hardware, and 25 dollars for the corner stakes and cardboard liner, not including the soil and compost fill.

## First season

Plant a cover crop or a fast-growing crop like lettuce in the first few weeks to check drainage and sun exposure before committing to a full season planting plan.
