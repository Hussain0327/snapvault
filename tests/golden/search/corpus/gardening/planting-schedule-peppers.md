# Pepper Planting Schedule - Zone 6b

This schedule covers starting, transplanting, and harvesting peppers, including a jalapeno bed, for the same Zone 6b garden with a last frost date of May 10.

## Starting seeds indoors

Peppers need a longer head start than tomatoes: sow seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before the last frost, meaning between March 1 and March 15 this year.
Germination is slower too, often taking 10 to 14 days even with a heat mat holding the soil at 27 degrees Celsius.

## Hardening off

Start hardening off seedlings in late April, since peppers are more sensitive to cold than tomatoes and can suffer stunted growth if exposed to temperatures below 13 degrees Celsius, even briefly.
Limit the first outdoor session to 45 minutes in filtered shade.

## Transplanting

Do not transplant peppers until soil temperature is reliably above 18 degrees Celsius, usually not until the last week of May in this garden, about ten days later than the tomato transplant date.
Space plants 45 centimeters apart, closer than tomatoes since pepper plants stay more compact.

## Feeding

Feed with a balanced fertilizer at transplanting, then reduce nitrogen once the first flowers appear in mid-June, similar to the tomato schedule.
Peppers are more sensitive to overwatering than tomatoes and should dry out slightly between waterings.

## Watering

Water about 2 centimeters per week, slightly less than the tomatoes need, and let the top few centimeters of soil dry between waterings to avoid root rot.

## Harvest

Jalapenos are ready to pick starting around 70 days after transplanting, typically in the second week of August in this garden, while the bell peppers in the same bed take closer to 80 days.
Peppers left on the plant longer turn red and taste sweeter, though yield per plant drops if too many are left to ripen fully.

## Notes

The jalapeno plants produced about 1.5 kilograms per plant last season, noticeably less than the tomatoes but with almost no blossom end rot issues.
