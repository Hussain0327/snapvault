# Tomato Planting Schedule - Zone 6b

This schedule covers starting, transplanting, and harvesting tomatoes for a Zone 6b garden, based on an average last frost date of May 10.

## Starting seeds indoors

Start tomato seeds indoors 6 to 8 weeks before the last frost date, which means sowing between March 15 and March 29 for this zone.
Keep the soil temperature at 24 to 27 degrees Celsius using a heat mat until germination, which typically takes 7 to 10 days.

## Hardening off

Begin hardening off seedlings about two weeks before the planned transplant date, starting with 1 hour of outdoor exposure and adding 30 minutes each day.
Bring seedlings inside if overnight temperatures drop below 10 degrees Celsius during this period.

## Transplanting

Transplant into the garden after May 17, a week past the average last frost date, to reduce the risk of a late cold snap.
Space plants 60 centimeters apart in rows spaced 90 centimeters apart, and bury the stem up to the first set of true leaves to encourage a stronger root system.

## Feeding

Apply a balanced fertilizer at transplanting, then switch to a low-nitrogen, high-potassium feed once flowering begins, typically around mid-June.
Too much nitrogen after flowering starts produces excess foliage at the expense of fruit.

## Watering

Water deeply twice a week rather than lightly every day, aiming for about 2.5 centimeters of water per week including rainfall.
Inconsistent watering is the most common cause of blossom end rot in this garden's tomatoes, based on three seasons of notes.

## Harvest

The first ripe tomatoes typically appear around July 20, roughly 65 to 75 days after transplanting depending on the variety.
Cherry tomato varieties ripen earliest, usually a full two weeks before the beefsteak varieties planted in the same bed.

## Notes

Last year's harvest peaked in early August with about 4 kilograms of tomatoes per plant from the six Roma plants in the north bed.
